## Title (working)

Quartz-enhanced photoacoustic detection of nitrous oxide at 7.7 µm using a
pulsed external-cavity quantum cascade laser

## Abstract (draft, ~250 words)

Nitrous oxide is the third most important anthropogenic greenhouse gas and
the dominant stratospheric ozone-depleting emission, motivating compact,
sensitive sensors for atmospheric and industrial monitoring. We report a
quartz-enhanced photoacoustic spectroscopy (QEPAS) sensor for N2O based on a
broadly tunable (1111–2000 cm⁻¹) pulsed-only external-cavity quantum cascade
laser and a commercial acoustic detection module. Because the source offers
no continuous-wave operation, wavelength-modulation (2f) detection is
precluded; the sensor instead uses 1f amplitude modulation, triggering the
laser at the tuning-fork resonance (12.4588 kHz, Q ≈ 9000) with
on-line/off-line background subtraction. The target R-branch transition of
the N2O ν1 fundamental near 1297 cm⁻¹ was located experimentally by a
photoacoustic step scan, compensating the tuning offset of the external-cavity
source, and gas attribution was verified by gas-exchange measurements.
