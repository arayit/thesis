\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\label{ch:background}

This chapter provides the background needed to follow the rest of the thesis:
the principle of photoacoustic spectroscopy, the QEPAS technique and its
transducer, the detection scheme used with a pulsed laser, the figures of merit
used to compare sensors, and the spectroscopy of \nto.

\section{Photoacoustic Spectroscopy}
\label{sec:pas}



Explain the photoacoustic effect: modulated optical absorption heats the gas
periodically, generating a pressure (acoustic) wave that a transducer detects.
Note that the signal is proportional to the absorbed optical power, so it scales
with laser power and with analyte concentration.

\section{Quartz-Enhanced Photoacoustic Spectroscopy (QEPAS)}
\label{sec:qepas}

Describe how QEPAS replaces the microphone with a quartz tuning fork acting as a
resonant piezoelectric transducer~\cite{kosterev2002}, optionally with an
acoustic micro-resonator (the ADM). Discuss the high quality factor $Q$, the
narrow resonance at $f_0$, and why this gives strong noise immunity and a small
sampling volume~\cite{patimisco2014}.

\section{Detection Scheme: Amplitude Modulation with a Pulsed QCL}
\label{sec:detection}

For a continuous-wave laser, wavelength modulation with second-harmonic (2f)
lock-in detection is common. With a \emph{pulsed} QCL, as used in this thesis,
the laser intensity is instead modulated at the tuning-fork resonance $f_0$
(amplitude modulation), and the photoacoustic signal is recovered with a lock-in
amplifier at the fundamental (1f). The absorption line is scanned by slowly
tuning the laser wavelength via temperature and/or drive current. Explain the
trade-offs of pulsed operation (e.g.\ spectral broadening, duty cycle) relative
to CW.

\section{Figures of Merit}
\label{sec:fom}

Define the metrics used later: signal-to-noise ratio, the normalized
noise-equivalent absorption (NNEA, in $\mathrm{cm^{-1}\,W\,Hz^{-1/2}}$),
minimum detectable concentration, and the use of the Allan deviation to find the
optimal integration time.

\section{Spectroscopy of Nitrous Oxide}
\label{sec:n2o-spectroscopy}

\nto\ has its strongest fundamental ($\nu_3$) near \SI{4.5}{\micro\meter}, but the
tunable quantum cascade laser used in this work (Daylight Solutions MIRcat-2300)
covers \SIrange{1111}{2000}{\per\centi\meter} (\SIrange{5}{9}{\micro\meter}).
The accessible \nto\ feature is therefore the $\nu_1$ fundamental band near
\SI{7.7}{\micro\meter}; the line used here is at \SI{1297.8}{\per\centi\meter},
with a secondary line at \SI{1271.5}{\per\centi\meter}. Line selection is made
with the HITRAN database~\cite{hitran2020}. A key practical issue in this region
is strong interference from water vapour (\water), which has many lines across
the \SIrange{1111}{2000}{\per\centi\meter} window (demonstrated in
\cref{ch:results}). Nitrogen, used as the buffer gas, is essentially
non-absorbing here.
