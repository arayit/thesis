\noindent\emph{Note: the values reported here are from preliminary

This chapter reports the experimental characterization of the sensor's
subsystems and the first \nto\ detection results, followed by the identification
of water-vapour interference, the observed pressure dependence, and a discussion
of the current performance and its limitations.

\section{Tuning-Fork / ADM Resonance Characterization}
\label{sec:adm-char}

The QTF resonance was located by driving the ADM modulation input with a
sinusoid from the waveform generator (\SI{100}{\milli\volt} peak-to-peak,
\SI{50}{\ohm}) and recording the amplified output on the oscilloscope while
sweeping the drive frequency in \SI{0.1}{\hertz} steps. Starting from the nominal
\SI{12459.2}{\hertz}, the response fell from \(\approx\)\SI{800}{\milli\volt} to
\(\approx\)\SI{400}{\milli\volt} within \SI{1}{\hertz}; sweeping downwards, a
clear maximum of \SI{930}{\milli\volt} (\SI{1.85}{\volt} peak-to-peak) was found at
\SI{12458.8}{\hertz}. The operating resonance frequency was therefore fixed at
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:f0}
  f_0 = \SI{12458.8}{\hertz}.
\end{equation}
This value is used as the QCL repetition (modulation) frequency throughout. \todo{Record a full frequency sweep around $f_0$ in fine (sub-Hz) steps to plot the ADM resonance curve and extract the quality factor $Q = f_0/\Delta f$; only a few points were taken so far, so no resonance figure is shown yet.}

\section{Lock-In Amplifier Characterization}
\label{sec:lockin-char}

The EG\&G 5110 lock-in was characterized at $f_0$ by varying the modulation
amplitude applied to the QTF and recording the demodulated magnitude $R$
(\cref{fig:lockin}). The response is approximately linear in the input amplitude
down to \(\approx\)\SI{4}{\milli\volt}, below which the oscilloscope trace loses
its sinusoidal shape and $R$ becomes unreliable.

A reproducible instrument artefact was also found: on the \SI{500}{\micro\volt},
\SI{5}{\milli\volt} and \SI{50}{\milli\volt} sensitivity ranges the analog output
reads \(\approx\)\SI{40}{\percent} higher than expected when the time constant is
\(\ge\)\SI{30}{\milli\second}, in both XY and $R$--$\theta$ modes; reducing the
time constant to \(\le\)\SI{10}{\milli\second} restores the expected value. All
other ranges are consistent. These three ranges are therefore avoided, or used
only with short time constants.

\section{QCL Power and Tuning Characterization}
\label{sec:qcl-char}

The average output power of the MIRcat-2300 was measured across its tuning range
for several pulse settings (\cref{fig:qcl-tuning}). The power varies strongly with
wavelength, from below \SI{1}{\milli\watt} near the band edges to a maximum of
\(\approx\)\SI{124}{\milli\watt} around \SIrange{7.5}{7.6}{\micro\meter}
(\SI{100}{\kilo\hertz}, \SI{200}{\nano\second}). At the \nto\ operating line
(\SI{1297.8}{\per\centi\meter}, \(\approx\)\SI{7.7}{\micro\meter}) the available
power is ample, and the working point used for detection was \(\approx\)\SI{20}{\milli\watt}
at \SI{1000}{\nano\second} pulse width. This tuning map also guides line
selection, since regions of low output power (e.g.\ near \SI{6.2}{\micro\meter})
cannot be probed effectively.

\section{Optical Power Budget Verification}
\label{sec:power-budget-results}

After alignment, the input and output powers were compared against the predicted
two-window transmission of \SI{87.2}{\percent} (\cref{sec:power-budget}). At
$\lambda = \SI{7.7}{\micro\meter}$ with a \SI{500}{\nano\second} pulse width,
$P_\text{in} = \SI{3.85}{\milli\watt}$ gave $P_\text{out} = \SI{3.15}{\milli\watt}$;
the window model predicts \SI{3.35}{\milli\watt}, leaving \(\approx\)\SI{0.2}{\milli\watt}
of additional loss inside the module. This small residual indicates the beam is
essentially clearing the prongs and resonator, confirming acceptable alignment.

\section{Detection of Nitrous Oxide}
\label{sec:n2o-detection}

\nto\ was first detected at \SI{1297.8}{\per\centi\meter} with the QCL at
\SI{1000}{\nano\second} / \SI{20}{\milli\watt}, \SI{100}{\ppm} \nto\ in a closed
chamber at \SI{500}{\torr}. The lock-in, in \%FS $R$--$\theta$ mode at
\SI{20}{\milli\volt} sensitivity, showed a stable photoacoustic signal of
\(\approx\)\SI{12}{\milli\volt}. Toggling the laser and the gas flow off and on
removed the signal in every case, confirming that the reading is a genuine
photoacoustic response to \nto\ and not an electrical or background artefact.

Two \nto\ lines were then compared (\SI{800}{\nano\second}, \SI{100}{\ppm},
\SI{600}{\torr}): \SI{1297.8}{\per\centi\meter} (\SI{22}{\milli\watt}) and
\SI{1271.5}{\per\centi\meter} (\SI{19}{\milli\watt}) gave \SI{14.0}{\milli\volt}
and \SI{15.5}{\milli\volt} respectively (\cref{tab:n2o}).

\section{Spectral Interference from Water Vapour}
\label{sec:h2o}

When the laser was tuned to a nominally non-absorbing region near
\SI{1400}{\per\centi\meter} as a control, strong signals were nonetheless
observed. Scanning the QCL revealed peaks at \num{1456.9}, \num{1405.2},
\num{1288.5} and \SI{1287.5}{\per\centi\meter} (\num{15.0}, \num{29.0},
\num{23.0} and \SI{28.3}{\milli\volt}), and further peaks near
\num{1798.5} and \SI{1772.7}{\per\centi\meter} (\num{16} and \SI{20}{\milli\volt}).
Cross-referencing with HITRAN~\cite{hitran2020} attributes these to water vapour
(\water) present in the chamber; nitrogen shows no absorption in this window.
Water-vapour interference is thus a significant practical issue for \nto\
detection in this spectral region (\cref{fig:h2o}) and motivates either gas
drying or careful line selection away from \water\ features.

\section{Pressure Dependence}
\label{sec:pressure}

The photoacoustic signal increased approximately linearly with chamber pressure
over the range explored. Purging with N\textsubscript{2} while pumping reduced
the pressure to \SIrange{60}{70}{\torr} and brought the lock-in reading to
essentially zero; whether this is due to the low pressure itself or to the
removal of residual absorbers (including \water) was not separated and remains an
open question. \todo{Add a controlled signal-vs-pressure measurement at fixed
concentration.}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

These results demonstrate a working pulsed-QCL QEPAS sensor that detects \nto\ at
the \SIrange{50}{100}{\ppm} level and a complete, characterized detection chain.
A background measurement highlights the main current limitation:
at the non-absorbing line \SI{1340}{\per\centi\meter} the lock-in read
\SI{1.08}{\milli\volt} (\SI{3.18}{\milli\watt}), rising only to
\SI{1.52}{\milli\volt} at \SI{1297.8}{\per\centi\meter} (\SI{3.23}{\milli\watt}).
The large wavelength-independent background --- despite an in-module loss of only
\(\approx\)\SI{0.2}{\milli\watt} --- points to residual beam clipping / wall
heating from imperfect alignment rather than window loss, and currently limits
the achievable detection limit.

The remaining work is to suppress this background (alignment, gas drying), and
then to convert the measured signals into quantitative figures of merit: a
concentration calibration curve, the normalized noise-equivalent absorption
(NNEA), the minimum detectable concentration, and an Allan-deviation analysis of
the optimal integration time. Water-vapour interference and the pressure
dependence should be characterized systematically as part of this.
