%==============================================================================
\chapter{Sensor Design and Experimental Setup}
\label{ch:methodology}
%==============================================================================

This chapter describes the QEPAS sensor built in this work: the pulsed quantum
cascade laser source, the acoustic detection module, the amplitude-modulation
detection and lock-in recovery scheme, the beam-delivery optics and alignment
procedure, the optical power budget, and the gas-handling system.

\section{System Overview}
\label{sec:system}

The sensor follows a standard QEPAS architecture (\cref{fig:setup}). A pulsed,
tunable quantum cascade laser (QCL) is tuned onto a \nto\ absorption line and
pulsed at the resonance frequency of a quartz tuning fork (QTF) housed in a
commercial acoustic detection module (ADM). The beam is steered and focused
between the QTF prongs by a two-mirror system. Periodic optical absorption by
\nto\ generates an acoustic wave that drives the QTF at resonance; the resulting
piezoelectric current is amplified by the ADM's integrated preamplifier and
demodulated by a lock-in amplifier referenced to the laser pulse train. A
mass-flow / pressure system sets the \nto\ concentration and chamber pressure.
The main parameters are listed in \cref{tab:params}.

\section{Excitation Source: Pulsed Quantum Cascade Laser}
\label{sec:qcl}

The excitation source is a Daylight Solutions MIRcat-2300 tunable QCL, capable
of emitting between \SI{1111}{\per\centi\meter} and \SI{2000}{\per\centi\meter}
(\SIrange{5}{9}{\micro\meter}). It is operated in pulsed mode and tuned onto an
\nto\ $\nu_1$ R-branch line located experimentally as described in
\cref{sec:line-location}. The laser is driven in
\emph{external-trigger} mode: a waveform generator (Rigol DG922\,Pro) supplies a
\SI{5}{\volt} (peak-to-peak) TTL pulse train at the QTF resonance frequency
$f_0 = \SI{12458.8}{\hertz}$ to the QCL \texttt{TRIG IN} input (high impedance,
pulse mode), so that one laser pulse is emitted per QTF oscillation period. This
constitutes the amplitude modulation of the optical power at $f_0$. Average
optical power is monitored with an Ophir Nova~II power meter.

\section{Spectral Line Location and Wavelength Calibration}
\label{sec:line-location}

The MIRcat wavelength readout has a specified absolute accuracy of
$\pm$\SI{0.5}{\per\centi\meter}, comparable to the spacing of adjacent \nto\
$\nu_1$ R-branch lines ($\approx$\,\SI{0.8}{\per\centi\meter}) and much larger
than the pressure-broadened linewidth at the operating pressure
($\approx$\,\SIrange{0.1}{0.2}{\per\centi\meter}). Setting the laser to the
nominal line-centre wavenumber therefore does not guarantee spectral overlap
with the target transition; indeed, initial measurements at a fixed nominal
wavenumber showed no discernible difference between pure N\textsubscript{2}
and \SI{100}{\ppm} \nto\ (\cref{sec:n2o-detection}).

The target line is therefore located experimentally by a step scan: with
\SI{100}{\ppm} \nto\ flowing, the laser is stepped in
\SI{0.1}{\per\centi\meter} increments (the instrument's set-point resolution)
over a $\approx$\,\SI{4}{\per\centi\meter} window centred on the nominal
target, with the pulse width, repetition rate and average power held constant.
At each step the lock-in output is allowed to settle for at least five time
constants before it is read. The photoacoustic signal exhibits a clear peak at
a nominal set point of \SI{1296.7}{\per\centi\meter}, i.e.\ offset by
\SI{-0.3}{\per\centi\meter} from the nominal target, within the specified
absolute accuracy. All subsequent measurements use this experimentally located
set point, and the scan is repeated at the start of each measurement campaign
to guard against slow drifts of the tuning calibration. Because the pulsed
laser linewidth ($<$\,\SI{1}{\per\centi\meter}) exceeds the line spacing, the
observed peak may comprise an unresolved group of adjacent R-branch
transitions; the assignment is discussed in \cref{sec:n2o-detection}.

\section{Acoustic Detection Module}
\label{sec:adm}

The transducer is a Thorlabs ADM01 acoustic detection module, comprising a
quartz tuning fork with an acoustic micro-resonator and an integrated
transimpedance preamplifier (powered at \(\pm\)\SI{12}{\volt} from a Keysight
E361A supply). The module has wedged, uncoated BaF\textsubscript{2} windows of
\(\approx\)\SI{2.5}{\milli\meter} thickness. The measured fork resonance is
$f_0 = \SI{12458.8}{\hertz}$ (\cref{sec:adm-char}). The ADM is mounted on a
five-axis translation stage (Thorlabs PY005) to allow the beam to be centred
between the fork prongs, whose gap is \(\approx\)\SI{0.8}{\milli\meter}.

\section{Amplitude-Modulation Detection and Lock-In Recovery}
\label{sec:am-scheme}

Because the QCL is pulsed, detection uses amplitude modulation rather than
wavelength-modulation: the pulse train at $f_0$ excites the QTF at resonance, and
the photoacoustic signal is recovered at the fundamental (1f) with an EG\&G 5110
lock-in amplifier connected to the ADM signal output. The lock-in reference is
derived from the QCL \texttt{TRIG OUT} port, which emits a \SI{5}{\volt} TTL
level while the laser is firing.

Two practical issues were addressed. First, below a pulse width of
\(\approx\)\SI{400}{\nano\second} the lock-in fails to lock to the
\texttt{TRIG OUT} reference (\texttt{UNLK} indicator). This is solved by using a
second waveform-generator channel as a \emph{dummy reference} with a fixed
\SI{500}{\nano\second} pulse width, phase-matched to \texttt{TRIG OUT} (a phase
offset of \(\approx\)\ang{217.4} compensates the QCL internal-electronics delay).
Second, at sensitivity settings of \SI{500}{\micro\volt}, \SI{5}{\milli\volt} and
\SI{50}{\milli\volt} the analog output reads \(\approx\)\SI{40}{\percent} high
for time constants \(\ge\)\SI{30}{\milli\second}; these settings are therefore
avoided (see \cref{sec:lockin-char}).

\section{Signal Validation and Background Characterization Protocol}
\label{sec:validation-protocol}

Pulsed QEPAS systems exhibit a non-zero background signal even in the absence
of the analyte, originating from optical absorption of the beam tails by the
module windows and resonator structure. The following protocol separates and
validates the signal contributions.

\paragraph{Electronic floor.} With the laser emission blocked, the lock-in
output is $\approx$\,\SI{0.1}{\milli\volt}, establishing that electrical
pick-up from the pulse electronics is negligible compared with all optical
signals.

\paragraph{Photoacoustic nature of the background.} With the laser on and no
analyte present, the background signal scales linearly with average optical
power (varied via the pulse width at fixed repetition rate;
$R^2 = 0.989$ over \SIrange{0.8}{5.4}{\milli\watt}), confirming a genuine
photoacoustic origin rather than electronic artefacts.

\paragraph{Gas attribution.} The analyte signal is attributed by (i) the
wavelength selectivity demonstrated in the step scan of
\cref{sec:line-location}, and (ii) gas-exchange (on/off) measurements at the
line centre, in which the chamber is alternately supplied with the certified
\nto\ mixture and pure N\textsubscript{2} at the same pressure and flow.

\paragraph{Background subtraction.} Because the structural background is
spectrally flat while the analyte absorption is a narrow line, quantitative
measurements use on-line/off-line subtraction: the net analyte signal is the
difference between the lock-in reading at the located line centre and at a
nearby off-line set point.

\paragraph{Daily reference.} Each measurement day begins with a fixed
reference measurement (located line centre, pure N\textsubscript{2},
\SI{500}{\nano\second} pulse width, \SI{3}{\second} time constant, fixed
sensitivity range), providing a day-to-day anchor for the background level and
overall system response. The lock-in display quantity (magnitude $R$ or
in-phase $X$) and sensitivity range are recorded with every data set, since
selected sensitivity ranges exhibit a documented output artefact
(\cref{sec:lockin-char}).

\section{Beam Delivery and Alignment}
\label{sec:alignment}

The QCL emits a collimated beam of waist $w_0 = \SI{2.5}{\milli\meter}$. A
gold-coated plane mirror steers the beam by \ang{90} into a gold-coated off-axis
parabolic (OAP) mirror (Thorlabs MPD149-M01, reflected focal length
$f = \SI{101.6}{\milli\meter}$), which focuses it into the ADM. For a Gaussian
beam, the focused waist is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:focus}
  w_0' \approx \frac{\lambda f}{\pi w_0} \approx \SI{100}{\micro\meter},
\end{equation}
at $\lambda = \SI{7.7}{\micro\meter}$, with a Rayleigh range
$z_R \approx \SI{4.1}{\milli\meter}$. The QTF is placed at the focus; at the
entrance window (\(\approx\)\SI{22.1}{\milli\meter} before the focus) the beam
diameter is \(\approx\)\SI{1.1}{\milli\meter}, comfortably below the
\(\approx\)\SI{1.6}{\milli\meter} clear aperture of the ADM.

Alignment proceeds by first making the beam parallel to the optical axis with the
plane mirror, then setting the OAP at the focal distance and orienting it to
inject the beam through the entrance window; the five-axis stage is then used to
maximise the transmitted power at the output window. Comparing input and output
power against the predicted window transmission (\cref{sec:power-budget})
confirms that the beam clears the prongs and resonator without clipping.

\section{Optical Power Budget}
\label{sec:power-budget}

The expected transmission through the BaF\textsubscript{2} windows is estimated
by combining the Fresnel reflection at each interface with Beer--Lambert bulk
absorption,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:window}
  T_\text{window} = (1-R)^2 \, e^{-\mu_a d},
  \qquad
  R = \left(\frac{n-1}{n+1}\right)^2 ,
\end{equation}
where $n$ is the BaF\textsubscript{2} refractive index from the Sellmeier
relation. At $\lambda = \SI{7.7}{\micro\meter}$, $n = 1.429$, giving
$R \approx 0.0312$ and a single-interface transmission of \(\approx\)\SI{96.9}{\percent}.
The bulk absorption coefficient $\mu_a \approx \SI{1.9e-3}{\per\milli\meter}$ is
obtained from the manufacturer's \SI{92.07}{\percent} transmission for a
\SI{10}{\milli\meter} sample. For the \SI{2.5}{\milli\meter} ADM windows this
yields $T_\text{window} \approx \SI{93.4}{\percent}$ per window, and, applying
\cref{eq:window} to both the entrance and exit windows,
$T_\text{total} \approx \SI{87.2}{\percent}$ (a \SI{12.8}{\percent} loss). This
wavelength-specific figure is used during alignment to distinguish genuine window
loss from clipping inside the module. The transmission measured after alignment
is $\approx$\,\SI{85}{\percent} (\cref{sec:power-budget-results}), consistent
with the predicted \SI{87.2}{\percent} window-only transmission combined with a
residual \SIrange{2}{3}{\percent} structural loss, bounding beam clipping inside
the module to the few-percent level.

\section{Gas Handling and Concentration Control}
\label{sec:gas}

The gas line uses two Alicat mass-flow controllers (MFCs): an MC-10SCCM-D
(\(\le\)\SI{10}{\sccm}) delivering the analyte (a certified \SI{100}{\ppm} \nto\
in N\textsubscript{2} cylinder) and an MC-200SCCM-D/5M delivering N\textsubscript{2}
buffer. Their outputs are combined and fed to an Alicat PCD-15PSIA pressure
controller that regulates the ADM chamber pressure (one port to the closed ADM,
one to a Vacuubrand MZ-2C-NT pump exhausting to the fume hood). Flows are set in
the FlowVision v2.1.3 software. The delivered concentration is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:conc}
  C = c_0 \, \frac{F_\text{\nto}}{F_\text{\nto} + F_{\mathrm{N_2}}},
\end{equation}
where $c_0 = \SI{100}{\ppm}$ is the cylinder concentration and $F$ are the flow
rates; equal flows therefore give \SI{50}{\ppm}. Chamber pressure was typically
\SIrange{500}{600}{\torr}.
