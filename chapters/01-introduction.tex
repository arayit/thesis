\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

Nitrous oxide (\nto) is one of the most important long-lived greenhouse gases. Its global warming potential is roughly \num{270} times that of \cotwo\ over a 100-year horizon, and it is now among the dominant ozone-depleting gases released by human activity~\needcite{exact GWP-100 value for N2O with source (e.g. the latest IPCC assessment) and the dominant-ozone-depleter claim (e.g. Ravishankara et al. 2009)}. Its atmospheric abundance has climbed well past \SI{330}{\ppb} and keeps rising year on year, driven mainly by human activity~\needcite{atmospheric N2O abundance and growth rate}. The largest anthropogenic sources are agriculture and soil management, followed by combustion and industrial processes~\needcite{breakdown of anthropogenic N2O sources or the global N2O budget}, while \nto\ also appears in medical anaesthesia and as a minor component of exhaled breath~\needcite{N2O in medical use and breath analysis}. Measuring \nto\ at trace levels, down to parts per million and below, therefore matters for climate science, for environmental regulation, and for health.

Detecting such low concentrations needs a sensor that is sensitive, selective, fast, and ideally compact. Laser absorption spectroscopy meets these requirements and has become a standard tool for trace-gas monitoring~\needcite{review of laser absorption spectroscopy for trace-gas sensing}, and among its variants quartz-enhanced photoacoustic spectroscopy (QEPAS) is a good fit for a compact instrument. It replaces the microphone of a conventional photoacoustic cell with a millimetre-scale quartz tuning fork that acts as a sharply resonant acoustic transducer, which gives strong immunity to environmental acoustic noise, needs only a very small gas volume, and offers a wide dynamic range~\cite{kosterev2002, patimisco2014}. Pairing this transducer with a quantum cascade laser (QCL) tuned to a strong \nto\ absorption line in the mid-infrared makes a compact and selective \nto\ sensor realistic.

The wider goal behind this work is the optical detection of trace volatile organic compounds (VOCs), such as those released by plants. \nto\ is used here as a well-behaved reference analyte, since it has strong and well-documented absorption lines that make it a good choice for building and benchmarking the sensor before moving on to more demanding targets.

\section{Problem Statement and Objectives}
\label{sec:objectives}

This thesis covers the design, construction, and experimental characterization of a QCL-based QEPAS sensor, operated with a pulsed QCL in amplitude-modulation mode, and its validation on \nto. The specific objectives are the following.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Assemble and align the optical, acoustic, and electronic parts of the QEPAS sensor.
  \item Characterize the key components, namely the quartz tuning fork and acoustic detection module (ADM), the lock-in detection chain, and the pulsed QCL.
  \item Establish an optical power budget for the beam-delivery path.
  \item Select a suitable \nto\ absorption line and demonstrate detection, including a concentration calibration.
  \item Quantify the sensor performance through its sensitivity (normalized noise-equivalent absorption), its minimum detectable concentration, and its stability from an Allan-deviation analysis.
\end{itemize}

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

The main contributions of this thesis are the following.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A complete pulsed-QCL QEPAS sensor, designed and integrated from a tunable quantum cascade laser, a quartz tuning fork detection module, and a lock-in detection chain into a single working instrument.
  \item An amplitude-modulation detection scheme for a pulsed QCL, together with the external-trigger and reference arrangement that lets the lock-in recover the signal reliably, including a fix for the loss of lock at short pulse widths.
  \item A full experimental characterization of the instrument, covering the tuning-fork resonance, the lock-in chain, the laser power and tuning, and a wavelength-specific optical power budget for the beam delivery.
  \item A demonstrated \nto\ detection that validates the complete instrument end to end and provides the foundation for future trace-VOC detection.
\end{itemize}
